## Research in context

### Evidence before this study
Allergic diseases (asthma, rhinitis and atopic dermatitis) affect more than a billion people and are conventionally framed as early-life immune programming. Yet allergy is common in older adults, and which *dimension* of ageing carries this susceptibility has never been formally adjudicated: molecular ageing is read by epigenetic clocks[1][2][3], functional ageing by deficit accumulation (frailty), and the two correlate only modestly. Genetic studies have treated ageing as a single quantity; allergic propensity has never been formalized as a quantitative process; and no candidate mediator has been triangulated across genetic, population, cellular and pharmacological layers. (This panel is a narrative synthesis of the cited literature rather than a systematic review; no formal database search with pre-specified terms was performed.)

### Added value of this study
Under a pre-specified adjudication scheme triangulating Mendelian randomization (MR), canonical genetic correlation, population and longitudinal cohorts, cell-type and single-cell analyses, drug-target genetics and pharmacovigilance (168 machine-readable additional files), we show that a functional frailty dimension (reproducibly not the clocks) shares architecture with allergic disease, preferentially with adult-onset asthma (rg 0.487 vs 0.209 for childhood-onset, P = 5.7 × 10⁻⁹). We formalize allergic propensity as a regulated haematological type-2 set-point that drifts 0.074 SD per decade in cross-sectional data (two survey cycles; 0.070 after birth-cohort adjustment), mildly accelerates after midlife, keeps intact damping, and carries an age-invariant allergic-risk gain confirmed against objective IgE for the full index (the objective layer is eosinophil carried); a target-trial emulation bounds the association against unmeasured confounding (OR 1.86, E-value 2.66). An outcome-wide coupling map (85 tests) generalizes the dimension law with clean boundaries. IL18 is established as an assay-robust, barrier-resident translational node (same three cis instruments across platforms; the genetic evidence is anchored to atopic dermatitis) whose full ATP-inflammasome maturation machinery co-localizes in lung macrophages, and a triple-filter screen of geroprotective compounds quantifies the barrier-immune cost of anti-ageing pharmacology (only metoprolol and LY-294002 pass; LINCS single-resource, and LY-294002 is a tool compound).

### Implications of all the available evidence
Allergy of older adults is the cumulative read-out of a lifelong, measurably drifting set-point that belongs to the functional ageing dimension and is visible in a routine blood count. Prevention can target a druggable inflammasome-IL-18 node, and the coming generation of anti-ageing therapeutics should be screened for barrier-immune cost, favouring combination (senolytic plus anti-alarmin) strategies over single-agent repurposing.

## Summary

**Background** Ageing is heterogeneous. Whether allergic susceptibility belongs to the molecular (epigenetic-clock) or the functional (deficit-accumulation) dimension is unknown, as is whether allergic propensity is a fixed trait or a drifting set-point.

**Methods** Five ageing instruments (four epigenetic clocks, one frailty index) and a blood-cell composite were tested against asthma, allergic rhinitis and atopic dermatitis (FinnGen R12) under a pre-specified decision tree, with independent GWAS replication and meta-analysis, MHC-free LDSC with formal dimension contrasts, multivariable and reverse MR, NHANES (14,878 participants) and HRS (45,234) cohorts with set-point models and target-trial emulation, 5q31 and cell-type colocalization, a donor-level single-cell atlas, cross-platform cis-pQTL MR with safety scanning, and LINCS pharmacological screening. Claims are graded adjudicated, nominal or hypothesis-generating.

**Findings** The frailty dimension, not the clocks, carried the genetic overlap with allergic disease (asthma rg 0.458-0.467; rhinitis 0.353-0.361; atopic dermatitis 0.246-0.250) and coupled to 12 of 17 endpoints at FDR q < 0.05, with an adult-onset preference (rg 0.487 vs 0.209, P = 5.7 x 10-9). A blood-cell composite predicted allergic outcomes (OR 1.42 per SD) and objective IgE, and behaved as a type-2 set-point whose level drifted 0.074 SD per decade in cross-sectional data (0.070 after birth-cohort adjustment), accelerated after midlife, kept intact damping, and carried an age-invariant risk gain (target-trial OR 1.86; E-value 2.66). A pQTL meta-analysis established IL18 as an assay-robust (OR 1.19, I2 = 0%; same three cis instruments across platforms; the genetic evidence is anchored to atopic dermatitis, European ancestry) barrier-resident node with a complete ATP-inflammasome machinery, and only 2 of 52 geroprotectors suppress ageing without amplifying the alarmin programme.

**Interpretation** Allergic susceptibility is a drifting functional-ageing set-point (cross-sectional, cohort-checked), not a clock-read molecular state, with a druggable inflammasome-IL18 node and a quantifiable barrier-immune cost for anti-ageing pharmacology; causality and functional validation remain to be shown.

## 1. Introduction

Ageing is measured two ways, and the two are not the same. Molecular ageing is read from epigenetic clocks built to predict chronological age or mortality; functional ageing is read from the accumulation of health deficits, frailty[4][5]. The two families correlate only modestly, respond differently to intervention[6][7], and may couple to different diseases. Allergic disease is an instructive test case for this partition. It is the most common chronic immune disorder of high-income countries[8][9]; it is classically attributed to early-life immune programming; and yet adult-onset disease is frequent[10], and the same low-grade inflammatory drift that marks ageing (inflammageing) tracks atopic morbidity[11][12]. Three mutually exclusive readings have never been adjudicated: allergic susceptibility could be constitutional (no ageing architecture at all), a clock phenomenon (molecular-age-driven), or a functional-dimension phenomenon (deficit-accumulation-driven).

The adjudication matters beyond taxonomy. A constitutional reading forecloses prevention through ageing biology. A clock reading points to methylation-modifying interventions. A functional reading predicts three things a clock reading does not: shared genetic architecture with deficit accumulation; a measurable drift of allergic propensity with age in ordinary physiology, rather than a stable constitutional level; and resolution onto druggable inflammatory nodes of barrier tissue. We therefore pitted five ageing instruments and a blood-cell composite against three allergic outcomes[13] under a pre-specified adjudication scheme, formalized the winning construct as a quantitative set-point process in population data (with explicit birth-cohort and period-effect decomposition), and carried the strongest molecular signal through cellular, mechanistic, knowledge-base and pharmacological layers. The design is deliberately adversarial throughout: negative controls, directionality audits, eosinophil-free reconstructions[14], ambient-RNA falsification, leave-one-instrument-out audits, and explicit claim grading (adjudicated, nominal, hypothesis-generating), so that each flagship statement survives or falls on its own terms.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Design, adjudication and claim grading
Five evidence layers chain genetic adjudication, population formalization, longitudinal context, cellular mechanism and translation (data resources: Table 1). The pre-specified decision tree distinguished a concordant multi-instrument ageing effect (path A), null clocks with a positive composite (path B), and a mixed pattern requiring dimension-specific narrowing (path C). Three claim grades are used: **adjudicated** (pre-specified criteria met, FDR control within declared families, and where applicable colocalization), **nominal** (P < 0.05 without family control or colocalization support), **hypothesis-generating** (descriptive/sensitivity tier). Analysis plans for the discovery (R5/R6) layers were specified in advance by file provenance and are archived; an OSF registration exists (https://osf.io/f4v79; registrations qm2hy and f9d3w) but is post hoc relative to the R5/R6 analyses, and later evidence layers (R7-R18, including the single-cell, cross-ancestry and pharmacological analyses) were added post hoc under internally pre-declared statistical families; no prospective external preregistration timestamp was obtained at analysis time, so the a priori property of the R5/R6 analyses rests on file provenance and repository archival. Deviations are logged; all code and 168 additional files are public and versioned.[15]

### 2.2 Genetic instruments and two-sample MR
Five ageing instruments were assembled from published GWAS: GrimAge acceleration (5 SNPs), Hannum (56), IEAA (133), PhenoAge (89) and a frailty index (66–71 by outcome), clumped at r² < 0.001, all mean F > 10; plus a composite haematological type-2 index (93 selection SNPs; the number entering each analysis varies with outcome-side allele-aware harmonisation and is reported per table, e.g. 88 SNPs in the FinnGen R12 two-sample MR, 101 in the Pan-UK Biobank type-2-spectrum MR, 94 in the Steiger audit, and 73/15 for the eosinophil-free/eosinophil-only decompositions; outcome-informed component selection, acknowledged[16]). Two-sample MR used IVW primarily with MR-Egger, weighted median, MR-PRESSO and MR-RAPS sensitivity[17][18][19][20], Steiger directionality filtering throughout, and FDR < 0.05 across the 15 discovery pairs. Reverse MR instrumented the three outcomes against blood-cell-trait principal components. Discovery used FinnGen R12[21][22]; replication used independent non-FinnGen GWAS (Han, TAGC, Neale, Budu-Aggrey)[23][24] with fixed-effect and DerSimonian-Laird meta-analysis. Multivariable MR adjusted for BMI, smoking initiation and education; instrument-content sensitivity removed canonical immune loci (± 1 Mb) and rebuilt a 13-SNP non-HLA instrument.

### 2.3 Genetic correlation, contrasts and the coupling map
MHC-free genetic correlations used three implementations, an in-house engine (simulation-calibrated, benchmarked against published estimates), an independent Python-3 engine, and canonical Bulik-Sullivan ldsc v1.0.1[25] (HapMap3, MHC-free weights, --not-M-5-50), with formal frailty-versus-clock rg contrasts (12 pairs, BH-FDR, two covariance calibres). The outcome-wide coupling map covers 5 instruments × 17 FinnGen endpoints (85 tests, BH-FDR) under the canonical engine.

### 2.4 NHANES: validation, set-point model and target-trial emulation
NHANES cycles D (2005–2006) and J (2017–2018) yielded 14,878[26][27] analysis-eligible participants in total; the cycle-specific analytic subsets were J for allergic outcomes (n = 7,021; 1,092 events) and D for IgE (n = 7,854); the pooled eligible total is not the arithmetic sum of these two outcome-specific subsets (Table 1 footnote), and the model-specific complete-case sets were smaller (set-point drift model n = 9,360; no-CRP sensitivity n = 9,510; target-trial emulation n = 4,403), each reported with its analysis; the participant flow is shown in Supplementary Fig. 1. Survey-weighted logistic models estimated per-SD odds ratios; pre-specified secondary analyses covered age interactions, age strata, IgE biomarkers, hinge models and mortality. The set-point model X = S(age) + ε, S(age) = S0 + β·(age−50)/10, λ(age) = λ0·e^(−δ·(age−50)/10) was fitted per cycle and pooled (strata/PSU clustering), with weighted-AIC shape comparison (linear/quadratic/hinge), a 300-replicate cluster bootstrap for knot location, residual-dispersion damping tests, and gain-invariance tests including log-IgE and high-IgE strata; minimal detectable interaction effects were computed at 80% power. The index was z standardized within each cycle (level comparisons across cycles therefore compare survey-weighted distributions, not absolute levels); CRP was measured by a conventional assay in cycle D and a high-sensitivity assay in cycle J, and a pre-specified no-CRP sensitivity (DRIFT02, Supplementary Table 121) re-estimated all period contrasts without the CRP component and decomposed the period × age interaction across index components. Because cross-sectional age gradients confound birth cohorts, a pre-specified decomposition (DRIFT01, Supplementary Table 120) tested the period level shift at the same age, the period × age interaction, a continuous birth-cohort main effect and its age interaction (birth year = survey-year midpoint − age), and same-age cross-cohort level differences across five age bands. The target trial emulated high- versus low-index assignment with stabilized IPTW (age, sex, BMI, smoking, race/ethnicity, family income-to-poverty ratio), survey weights and PSU clustering, reporting E-values and per-SD sensitivity.

### 2.5 HRS longitudinal analyses
In 45,234 HRS participants (723,744 person-waves)[28], a deficit-accumulation index was tracked against mortality and incident chronic disease (time-fixed and time-varying models), and early-warning signals (within-person variance, lag-1 autocorrelation) were tested against approaching death. HRS contains no allergy outcomes or genotypes; it validates the clinical trajectory of the frailty construct only.

### 2.6 Mechanism: 5q31, cell-type MR, methylome, single-cell
Bayesian colocalization at 5q31[29][30] (RAD50–IL13–IL4–IL5) contrasted whole-blood (eQTLGen) and DICE cell-type cis-eQTLs[31][32] against the three outcomes, with conditional multi-signal follow-up, shadow masking, SMR/HEIDI[33], colocalization under a baseline prior (p1 = p2 = 1×10⁻⁴, p12 = 1×10⁻⁵) with prior sweeps and allelic-direction analysis. Cell-type MR ran 97 powered DICE tests (FDR < 0.05). Methylome overlap of ageing- and allergy-associated CpGs was tested genome-wide and in blood (permutation, Fisher exact). The Tabula Sapiens 2.0 Immune (592,317 cells; 24 donors)[34][35] and Lung (65,847 cells) atlases were analysed with the donor as inferential unit under pre-declared families (five-test Treg; four-test translational) and a pre-specified ambient-RNA falsification. OneK1K served as an independent-instrument layer[36][37].

### 2.7 Proteome MR, triangulation, safety and pharmacovigilance
cis-pQTL instruments (inflammatory-protein meta-analysis[38]; all F > 10) were screened against the three outcomes (FDR < 0.1), requiring colocalization PP.H4 > 0.8, with positive and negative controls. The pQTL exposure cohorts overlap FinnGen R12 at most marginally (declared in the Supplementary Information), and the cross-platform replication shares the same three cis instruments throughout. IL18 and TNFSF14 were replicated across SCALLOP, Luminex and HELIC/AGES/INTERVAL platforms[39][40][41][42] (per-SNP Wald, fixed-effect IVW, cross-platform meta-analysis). A targeted safety scan covered ten non-allergic endpoints. Gene-level triangulation combined cis-eQTL MR, approximate colocalization, PRISM viability and ChEMBL mechanisms. Pharmacovigilance computed reporting odds ratios for anti-type-2 biologics across FAERS and Canada Vigilance.

### 2.8 Knowledge-base and pharmacological integration
The 17-gene type-2/alarmin panel (IL4, IL5, IL13, TSLP, IL33, IL18, TNFSF14, RORC, IL6, TNF, GATA3, STAT6, IL1R1, IL1RL1, IL5RA, IL25, IL17RB; the panel is defined in KB01, Supplementary Table 162) was tested for enrichment among 500 curated human ageing genes (Aging Atlas; hallmark-stratified). LINCS L1000 connectivity was computed for ageing (curated senescence + SASP union) and alarmin (IL33, IL25, IL4, IL5, IL13) signatures. The geroprotector triple filter required curated geroprotective evidence, significant ageing-signature suppression (q < 0.1, z < 0), and no alarmin upregulation. ATP-axis cis-eQTL MR (P2RX7, NLRP3, CASP1, GSDMD, IL1B) used eQTLGen whole blood (Z-standardized effects; 1000 Genomes EUR clumping, r² < 0.1, F ≥ 10), Wakefield-ABF colocalization, leave-one-instrument-out diagnostics, and a GTEx v8 EUR lung sensitivity[43].

### 2.9 Multiple testing and statistical families
Statistical families were pre-declared and controlled within family by Benjamini-Hochberg FDR. (i) Discovery two-sample MR: 15 instrument-outcome pairs (5 instruments x 3 outcomes), FDR < 0.05. (ii) Genetic-correlation contrasts: 12 frailty-versus-clock contrasts (4 clocks x 3 outcomes). (iii) Outcome-wide coupling map: 85 tests (5 instruments x 17 endpoints), FDR < 0.05. (iv) Cell-type MR: 97 powered DICE tests. (v) cis-pQTL screen: inflammatory proteins against three outcomes, FDR < 0.1, with colocalization PP.H4 > 0.8 required. (vi) ATP-axis cis-eQTL MR: 15 tests (5 genes x 3 outcomes). (vii) Regulatory-T-cell single-cell family: five tests (IL4 primary; IL13, GATA3, FOXP3, IL5 context). (viii) Translational single-cell family: four tests (IL18, TNFSF14 x two compartments). (ix) Pharmacological (LINCS/PRISM) families: FDR within signature. Results not surviving family control are labelled nominal (P < 0.05) or hypothesis-generating elsewhere in the text.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Adjudication: a functional dimension, not the clocks, tracks allergy
No outcome met path A; all were adjudicated path C, narrowing the association to the functional dimension. The frailty instrument was uniformly positive, asthma OR 3.44 per SD (z = 14.6, P = 3.5 × 10⁻⁴⁸), atopic dermatitis OR 1.46 (P = 3.2 × 10⁻¹²), rhinitis OR 1.69 (P = 2.2 × 10⁻¹²), robust across the estimator battery and MR-RAPS (Figure 1a; Table 2). The four clocks were weak and directionally split[1][2][3], GrimAge (OR 1.12) and Hannum (1.02) weakly positive, but IEAA significantly *protective* for asthma in both discovery and replication (OR 0.986, P = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵; meta OR 0.990, P = 5.9 × 10⁻⁵; Supplementary Table 1, Supplementary Table 58) and PhenoAge protective in discovery (OR 0.945) though attenuating in the replication meta (P = 0.081); because clock instruments are underpowered for small effects, this adjudicates asymmetry across dimensions, not absence of clock effects. The composite index showed concordant, smaller effects (asthma OR 2.32; atopic dermatitis 1.90; rhinitis 1.62; discovery two-sample MR — the phenome-wide scan in §3.3 reports marginally different composite estimates of 2.34, 1.88 and 1.63 from a different outcome-side SNP set). Because the frailty instrument is HLA dominated (82% of its genome-wide-significant SNPs in the HLA window), it carries direction only; magnitude claims attach to the composite. A per-SNP Steiger audit (Supplementary Table 135) shows that this magnitude-carrying composite is also the least directionally resolvable (only 37% of its SNPs explain more variance in the index than in asthma; 19 Steiger-significant SNPs point in the wrong direction versus 3 correct, binomial P = 0.017), the expected signature of blood-cell SNPs resident in allergy loci; the *direction* of the dimension effect therefore rests on the MHC-free genetic correlation (3.1) and the non-HLA instrument (below), not on the composite's individual SNPs. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST1-ST13, ST57-ST65, ST76-ST79, ST103, ST117, ST125-ST126, ST131, ST134-ST138.

Independent replication established direction and robustness: asthma (Han OR 2.25; TAGC 1.58; Neale 1.08; meta OR 2.39, I² = 90%), rhinitis (Neale 1.15; random-effects P = 0.087, I² = 96%), atopic dermatitis (Budu-Aggrey 2.66; meta 1.97, P = 0.024). Multivariable MR preserved the frailty effect (asthma OR 2.68, P = 1.9 × 10⁻⁹; atopic dermatitis 1.79, P = 8.9 × 10⁻⁸); removing every canonical immune locus left estimates unchanged (3.43 vs 3.44); and a 13-SNP non-HLA instrument retained the association across cohorts (FinnGen asthma OR 3.85; Pan-UK Biobank 2.38). Reverse effects were uniformly small (largest OR 1.024, ~ninefold smaller than forward on the log scale, descriptive). Two further ageing exposures (telomere length, longevity) reproduced no concordant signal, and an external non-HLA healthspan instrument was nominally direction-protective for asthma only (Supplementary Table 132), a third independent exposure bounding the dimension-specificity claim.

Genome-wide, MHC-free genetic correlation gave strong frailty correlations[44][45] (in-house: asthma rg 0.458, SE 0.038; rhinitis 0.353, SE 0.046; atopic dermatitis 0.250, SE 0.033; all q < 10⁻¹³; canonical: 0.467, 0.361, 0.246) versus predominantly null clock correlations, reproduced by an independent Python-3 engine and by canonical ldsc (median absolute difference 0.025), with MHC-included references shifting frailty rg by only 0.019–0.033. Formal contrasts confirmed the frailty correlation exceeded every clock correlation for asthma (differences 0.29–0.38; q ≤ 4.3 × 10⁻⁴) and rhinitis (0.32–0.40; q ≤ 1.4 × 10⁻³), reproduced under the canonical engine; the atopic-dermatitis contrast is convention dependent and reported as unresolved.

### 3.2 The coupling law, mapped: 85 tests with clean boundaries
The outcome-wide coupling map (Figure 2a) generalized the adjudication: the frailty instrument coupled to 12 of 17 endpoints at FDR q < 0.05, strongest in the asthma spectrum (asthma-NAS rg 0.637; asthma 0.467; rhinitis 0.361; chronic rhinosinusitis 0.154), extending to depression (0.548), pneumonia (0.431), myocardial infarction (0.395), sepsis (0.376), rheumatoid arthritis (0.374), psoriasis (0.292), atopic dermatitis (0.246) and femoral fracture (0.133), with sharp boundaries: type-1 diabetes, food allergy, ulcerative colitis, drug-induced anaphylaxis and dementia all null. The clocks were nearly silent on the three primary allergic endpoints (Hannum and IEAA null throughout; GrimAge and PhenoAge reached FDR significance only for the asthma-nasal-polyp endpoint, rg 0.209 and 0.212, q = 0.012 and 0.009; GrimAge additionally coupled to pneumonia, rg 0.413; note that GrimAge's correlation with asthma itself is significant in the 15-test discovery family (rg 0.17, q = 0.034) but not in this 85-test coupling family (q = 0.052), and we treat the 85-test family as primary for the coupling map while reporting the discrepancy explicitly): clocks index a real but differently coupled dimension, and allergic susceptibility does not live in it. One caveat protects the map from its own logic: for endpoints that share deficit content with the frailty measure itself (e.g., depression, femoral fracture), part of the correlation may reflect construct overlap rather than purely shared genetic architecture; the allergy conclusions rest on endpoints with no such overlap. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST119.

### 3.3 Adult-onset preference and phenome specificity
On onset-stratified asthma genetics, the frailty dimension shared more than twice as much architecture with adult-onset as with childhood-onset disease (rg 0.487 vs 0.209; conservative contrast z = 5.83, P = 5.7 × 10⁻⁹); clocks were null. The contrast is not attributable to power asymmetry: the childhood-onset GWAS is not underpowered (h² 0.0669, z = 6.9, mean χ² 1.45; adult-onset h² 0.0392, z = 11.4, mean χ² 1.28; childhood h² is in fact higher), so the adult preference reflects genuinely differential architecture[46] (Figure 1h; ONSET01, Supplementary Table 118). In the 17-endpoint phenome scan, the composite index was specific to allergic endpoints (asthma OR 2.34, q = 7.7 × 10⁻⁵; atopic dermatitis 1.88; rhinitis 1.63; asthma-nasal polyp 2.00, q = 8.6 × 10⁻⁸) with a single inverse association (myocardial infarction) and nulls elsewhere, whereas the frailty instrument showed implausible HLA-driven magnitudes at autoimmune endpoints (outlier-sensitive), confirming that its interpretable content is dimensional, not endpoint-specific (Supplementary Fig. 2-3). In independent UK Biobank endpoints the index associated with asthma (OR 2.78) and rhinitis (OR 1.58) and was null for urticaria and non-type-2 controls. In Biobank Japan, the rebuilt index was direction consistent[47] for asthma (β = 0.38, q = 0.048) and rhinitis (β = 0.52, q = 0.040) but estimator-sensitive; the atopic-dermatitis arm was direction discordant and formally non-adjudicable (below the cross-population heritability threshold). Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST74-ST75, ST80-ST82, ST94-ST98, ST107-ST111, ST118, ST130, ST132.

### 3.4 The set-point: age-invariant risk on a level that drifts 0.074 SD per decade
The composite index is a drifting functional set-point (Figure 3a; Table 3). In NHANES it predicted prevalent allergic outcomes (Figure 4a; OR 1.42 per SD; 1.76 per unit; 1.43 in stratum-fixed-effects), with weighting shifting estimates by +8.5% (below the pre-specified 30% materiality threshold), and it predicted objective IgE biomarkers[48] (Figure 4e; total IgE β = 0.310; high IgE OR 1.53; any specific IgE OR 1.39). The component decomposition qualifies this objective layer (Supplementary Table 124): the IgE signal is carried by the eosinophil component (eosinophil-only index: log-IgE β = 0.351, P = 6 × 10⁻²⁵; high-IgE OR 1.53), whereas the eosinophil-free index is null for IgE (β = 0.005, P = 0.88; high-IgE OR 1.04), the objective-IgE validation therefore confirms the construct validity of the full type-2 index rather than eosinophil-independence. The age-by-index interaction was null with adequate power (P = 0.394; 80% power for a 40-year OR ratio ≥ 1.134). Formalized as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck homeostat[49] (Supplementary Fig. 4), the set-point level drifted upward at 0.074 SD per decade (95% CI 0.060–0.088, P = 1.2 × 10⁻²⁵), reproduced in both survey cycles (D 0.056; J 0.089; the two cycles differ descriptively, as expected under period/cohort composition), robust to BMI adjustment (0.066); drift was near linear with a mild late acceleration (hinge ΔAIC = 18.3; knot median 55 years, IQR 55–60, boundary rate 0.04; slope 0.039 → 0.137 SD per decade, ≈3.5-fold, increment P = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴) and no plateau. Damping was intact: residual dispersion did not rise with age (δ = 0.000, P = 0.99; hinge-residual confirmation all P ≥ 0.10); a U-shaped dispersion profile (Brown-Forsythe P = 0.007) is reported without a resilience claim, and the critical-slowing signature documented in HRS decedents is explicitly not visible in cross-sectional adult dispersion. Gain invariance extended to the objective layer: no age interaction for log-IgE (P = 0.14) and a nominally reverse interaction in the high-IgE stratum (β = −0.062, P = 0.021). Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST14-ST21, ST70-ST73, ST112, ST120-ST121, ST124, ST153-ST155, ST17b, ST154b.

Because cross-sectional age gradients confound birth cohorts, we decomposed period and cohort effects directly (Figure 3; DRIFT01, Supplementary Table 120). There was no period level shift at the same age (cycle J vs D: β = 0.017, P = 0.60), and same-age cross-cohort level differences were small and unsystematic across five age bands (≤0.04 SD in four bands; the oldest band, 65–75, differed by +0.18 SD with the later-born cohort higher, the direction opposite to a cohort-inflated age gradient). The drift rate was steeper in the later period (interaction 0.033, P = 0.012), again inconsistent with an early-born-high-value artefact. With continuous birth-cohort main and interaction terms adjusted, the age drift was 0.070 SD per decade (95% CI 0.050–0.090, P = 9.2 × 10⁻¹²), attenuating the unadjusted estimate by only ~5%. Two cycles twelve years apart cannot fully separate age, period and cohort, but every decomposition direction argues against cohort inflation of the drift. A pre-specified assay-robustness check (DRIFT02, Supplementary Table 121) addressed the one remaining measurement caveat, cycle D used conventional CRP and cycle J high-sensitivity CRP: on the no-CRP index the steeper-in-J interaction persisted (0.041, P = 0.002), and component decomposition showed the interaction is carried by blood-count components (total WBC +0.037, P = 3.6 × 10⁻⁴; eosinophils +0.027; lymphocytes −0.027) while the CRP component itself runs in the opposite direction (−0.052, P = 4.4 × 10⁻⁴), the period slope difference is therefore not an assay artefact, and the no-CRP pooled drift (0.069 SD per decade) matches the primary estimate.

### 3.5 Drift decomposition: a pan-haematological myeloid skew
The drift is not allergy-specific (Figure 3f; Supplementary Fig. 5). Lymphocyte fraction fell (−0.077 SD/decade, P = 7.0 × 10⁻²⁷), monocyte fraction rose (+0.081, P = 4.7 × 10⁻²⁴), total WBC was near-null (−0.013 SD/decade, P = 0.013), the textbook myeloid-skew signature of haematological ageing, and eosinophil-only and neutrophil-only indices drifted comparably (0.039, P = 1.8 × 10⁻⁸ vs 0.044, P = 8.8 × 10⁻¹⁰), with an equal-weight index reproducing the drift (0.068). The type-2 specificity of the composite therefore resides in its allergic-risk gain, not in the drift itself. Construct-overlap controls converged for self-reported allergy but not for objective IgE: an eosinophil-free index retained the self-reported association (OR 1.63, P = 3.7 × 10⁻¹⁷) while eosinophil percentage alone did not (OR 1.07, P = 0.12), yet the same eosinophil-free index was null for IgE (β = 0.005, P = 0.88; Supplementary Table 124). The self-reported association is therefore type-2-generic, whereas the objective-IgE association is eosinophil carried, two endpoints probing different components rather than a single eosinophil-independent gain. At the genetic layer, removing eosinophil-percentage SNPs retained atopic dermatitis (OR 2.02, P = 2.0 × 10⁻⁹) but attenuated asthma (cohort-dependent: FinnGen OR 1.42, random-effects P = 0.45; Pan-UK Biobank OR 1.86, P = 1.7 × 10⁻⁷) and nulled rhinitis (OR 0.80; replicated null in Pan-UK Biobank), the eosinophil-carrying reading holds for rhinitis, atopic dermatitis retains a non-eosinophil component, and the asthma attribution remains unresolved. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST89, ST124, ST133, ST136, ST156.

### 3.6 Emulating a trial and bounding confounding
The target-trial emulation reproduced the survey association under a causal-inference design (Figure 4g; Supplementary Fig. 6): high versus low index gave OR 1.86 (income-adjusted primary; 1.90 without income; per-SD 1.45; E-value 2.66 on the prevalence-corrected risk-ratio scale, confidence limit 1.94; the odds ratio is converted to a risk ratio at the 15.6% outcome prevalence, Supplementary Table 146). The emulated outcome is the asthma-anchored allergic composite available in the merged extract; rhinitis- and AD-specific emulations were not performed. Smoking pack-years were unavailable; the estimate is bounded, not randomized. Drug-exposure subgroups (mTOR, methotrexate, JAK, IL-6 pathways; exposed n = 0–25) are reported as underpowered. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST146, ST152.

### 3.7 Longitudinal trajectory of the frailty construct (HRS)
In 45,234 HRS participants (Figure 4h; Supplementary Fig. 7) the deficit-accumulation index rose from 0.187 to 0.261 over follow-up and predicted mortality (HR 1.61 per SD; time-varying model 1.85) and incident chronic disease (HRs 1.16–1.77); early-warning signals (rising within-person variance and lag-1 autocorrelation) preceded death[49] and persisted under mean-index adjustment (the mortality analysis set comprised 36,528 participants with 18,276 deaths). HRS measures no allergy: this layer validates that the functional dimension is dynamic, prognostic, and shows end-of-life resilience loss, the same dimension whose genetic architecture carries allergic susceptibility. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST83-ST86, ST104-ST105.

### 3.8 Mechanism I: the IL4 paradox resolves at 5q31, in a cell-type-specific way
Whole-blood IL4 and allergic GWAS signals are driven by distinct variants (Figure 5a; Supplementary Fig. 8-9; PP.H3 ≈ 0.97–1.00 for all outcomes): the bulk signal is a linkage artefact, and the classic risk allele at rs2070874 lowers whole-blood IL4, a tissue-discordant effect not expected to be disease relevant. In naive regulatory T cells (DICE), IL4 colocalized[32] with atopic dermatitis (full-region PP.H4 = 0.92; multi-signal matched-pair 0.95) and, suggestively, with asthma (0.79; matched-pair 0.86; falls below 0.8 at the strictest prior), while the rhinitis signal was single-SNP driven and collapsed under masking. Cell-type MR across DICE compartments gave 33 of 97[32][50] powered tests at FDR < 0.05: TSLP and IL5 expression increased risk across T-cell and NK compartments, and IL4 in naive regulatory T cells was protective (asthma OR 0.92; atopic dermatitis 0.88). An independent OneK1K-instrument analysis implicated an adjacent gene set[36][37] (TNF, IL6, GATA3, RORC) rather than corroborating the DICE pairs, the IL4 regulatory-T-cell reading therefore remains nomination grade. Methylome analysis showed ageing- and allergy-associated CpGs[48] anti-enriched genome-wide but enriched in blood (Fisher OR 1.71), with type-2 genes enriched 55-fold in the overlap: the axis is written into the allergic epigenome of circulating cells (cross-sectional, direction-unresolved). Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST22-ST40, ST66-ST69, ST87-ST88, ST102, ST114-ST115, ST139b.

### 3.9 Mechanism II: the single-cell layer, drifting Treg IL5, barrier-resident IL-18
In the 24-donor immune atlas (Figure 6a[34][35]; Supplementary Fig. 10-12), regulatory-T-cell IL5 rose with donor age (equal-donor-weighted rate ratio 1.72 per decade, 95% CI 1.15–2.58, P = 0.025; leave-one-donor-out stable; nominal within the five-test family, q = 0.13); IL4 was directionally concordant but failed the pre-specified ambient-RNA falsification (plasma-cell proxy ρ = 0.59, P = 0.045; IL5 shows no such correlation, P = 0.11) and was downgraded. In the lung atlas (3 donors aged 59–61[51]; descriptive localization, not a population estimate; Supplementary Fig. 13-14), IL18 localized to alveolar macrophages (75%) and alveolar type-1 (71%), type-2 (55%) and basal (59%) epithelium, with IL33 confirming the basal-epithelial source (62%); after correction of a mis-mapped probe (documented erratum), true TSLP showed basal 15.4% / AT2 3.6%. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST90-ST93, ST116, ST116b, ST139-ST145.

### 3.10 Translation I: IL18, assay-robust and safety-mapped
Four cis-pQTL associations passed FDR < 0.1 (all for atopic dermatitis; Figure 7a; Table 4); colocalization retained IL18 (PP.H4 = 0.968) and TNFSF14 (0.976) and rejected TNF and TRAIL as linkage artefacts. IL18 replicated across three platforms (SCALLOP OR 1.19, P = 7.0 × 10⁻⁹; Luminex 1.16, P = 7.8 × 10⁻⁹; HELIC 1.22, P = 1.1 × 10⁻⁸; pooled OR 1.19, 95% CI 1.15–1.23, P = 3.6 × 10⁻²³, I² = 0%), and is targetable by the IL-18-binding protein tadekinig alfa[52][53]. Replication is cross-cohort and cross-assay using the same three cis instruments throughout, so independence is at the assay/cohort level and not at the instrument level; the discovery meta-analysis[38] is the SCALLOP Consortium Olink study, and two of the nominal replication layers share its infrastructure (INTERVAL is a constituent cohort; SCALLOP is the same consortium), whereas HELIC, AGES and the Ahola-Olli Luminex cohort are separate resources (Supplementary Information); the pooled three-platform estimate therefore mixes partially overlapping and independent layers and is reported as cross-assay consistency rather than fully independent replication, and the target genetic evidence is anchored to the atopic-dermatitis (European) arm. TNFSF14 replicated in AGES SomaScan (OR 1.20)[41] but is single-instrument dependent (rs413141, 71% weight). The targeted safety scan associated genetically proxied IL18 with psoriasis and femur fracture, directionally concordant with its risk effect, predicting no adverse signal from IL18 blockade at the scanned endpoints, whereas TNFSF14's inverse septicaemia and fracture associations flag infection and bone health for surveillance. Molecular mediation (Supplementary Fig. 15) found no systemic circulating cascade, and a gene-burden lookup found the alarmin IL33 protective across all three diseases[54][55], an instructive split within the alarmin family itself: IL33 protective, IL18 risk, which is precisely the genetic counterpoint that makes IL18 the specific node rather than a generic alarmin effect. Pharmacovigilance gave cross-database-consistent infection signals[56][57] for mepolizumab, omalizumab and tezepelumab, no replicated malignancy signal, and an omalizumab-only immunosenescence flag reported for confounder-adjusted interpretation. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST41-ST56, ST99, ST101, ST106, ST113, ST127-ST129.

### 3.11 Translation II: gene triangulation and the ATP-axis machinery map
Gene-level triangulation assembled RORC[14] (cis-eQTL MR OR 0.66, P = 0.007; approximate PP.H4 0.96) and IL6 (OR 1.40, P = 0.044; PP.H4 0.90) with three independent evidence lines each (MR + colocalization + PRISM/ChEMBL), alongside IL4 (OR 0.58, P = 1.3 × 10⁻⁸, PP.H4 ≈ 1.0, nomination-grade). The ATP-axis machinery map completed the cellular picture (Figure 7g; Supplementary Fig. 16): the full IL-18 maturation chain co-localizes in lung macrophages (IL18 75.3%, CASP1 64.9%, GSDMD 47.0%, P2RX7 46.4%, IL1B 40.8%; NLRP3 7.6%, dropout-limited; three donors aged 59–61, descriptive localization), while epithelium holds IL18 with almost no machinery, a myeloid-produce, epithelial-store two-compartment model. The genetic boundary is recorded with equal clarity: cis-eQTL MR for all five machinery genes against the three endpoints (15 tests) yielded no MR-plus-colocalization pair (all PP.H4 ≤ 0.08); three nominal signals survived leave-one-instrument-out audits with robust directions (NLRP3 × asthma OR 1.11, P = 0.018, q = 0.088; GSDMD × rhinitis OR 0.87, q = 0.022, PP.H4 = 0.018, direction concordant in GTEx lung; IL1B × AD OR 1.49, q = 0.053, single-SNP-weighted), and a chain-level mirror pattern (asthma signal in the NLRP3–CASP1–IL1B maturation chain; rhinitis signal in the P2RX7/GSDMD sensor-pore end) is logged as observation only, a pre-specified sign test on the a priori-defined chains is not significant (P = 0.25 and 0.5), and reported strictly as a falsifiable prior for perturbation experiments, not as causal evidence. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST100, ST147-ST148, ST157-ST161.

### 3.12 Translation III: knowledge convergence and the geroprotector reality map
The 17-gene type-2/alarmin panel was 12-fold enriched among 500 curated human ageing genes (4/17: IL1R1, IL6, TNF, TNFSF14; OR 12.1, P = 7.1 × 10⁻⁴ against a ~20,018-gene protein-coding universe; NF-κB module, human n = 47, OR 59.1, P = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴), a third, expert-curated layer converging with the genetic and epigenomic layers, upgrading TNFSF14 to a dual allergy-ageing node. The membership and enrichment are now deposited with their source file (Figure 8f; KB01, Supplementary Table 162; Aging Atlas `gene_sets_all.csv`) so the number is independently reproducible. IL18 appears in no Aging Atlas module (KB01): the field's newest ageing-implicated node is not in the field's own catalogue. LINCS connectivity had shown senolytic-class compounds (dasatinib, ruxolitinib, tofacitinib, navitoclax, quercetin) suppressing an internal ageing signature (q ≤ 0.033) but none suppressing both signatures (mTOR inhibitors null on both; a previously reported mTOR-alarmin signal did not replicate and was formally retracted). The curated-signature triple filter sharpened this to a quantitative map (Figure 8a; GER01, Supplementary Table 122; Supplementary Fig. 9): of 52 geroprotective compounds matchable in LINCS, only metoprolol (z = −2.78, q = 0.022) and LY-294002 (z = −2.22, q = 0.068) suppress the curated ageing signature without amplifying the alarmin programme; quercetin fails the curated filter (z = −0.39, q = 0.76) despite suppressing the internal signature, documenting signature-definition dependence; the strongest connectivities are amplifications (geldanamycin +7.33; loratadine +6.85; resveratrol +5.54; trametinib +4.43; digoxin +4.20; all q < 3 × 10⁻⁴). Three boundary statements keep this map honest: metoprolol rests on LINCS alone (absent from the PRISM nominations); LY-294002 is a tool compound, not a clinical drug; and the apparent tension between "no drug suppressed both (internal) signatures" and "two compounds pass the (curated) triple filter" is entirely a signature-definition difference, now tabulated side by side (GER02, Supplementary Table 123). Within the internal-definition screen, one compound stands out as the nearest single-molecule approximation of the combination logic: azithromycin suppresses both signatures in the same direction (ageing z = −2.17, marginal; alarmin z = −2.72, q = 0.024), hypothesis-generating, but with a well-known airway anti-inflammatory clinical background. The two positives define the combination logic rather than compete: metoprolol is the prioritizable geroprotector-class candidate, azithromycin the prioritizable anti-alarmin-class candidate. Supporting data: Supplementary Tables ST122-ST123, ST149-ST151, ST162.

## 4. Discussion

This study adjudicates a long-open question and then formalizes the answer. **First, the dimension law**: allergic susceptibility belongs to the functional (deficit-accumulation) dimension of ageing, not to molecular clocks. The result is adjudicated under a pre-specified tree [13] and reproduced by three LDSC implementations [25]. It is MHC-free by construction, extends across 85 coupling tests with explicit boundaries, and is sharpened by an adult-onset preference (rg 0.487 vs 0.209) that aligns the genetics with the clinical epidemiology of late-onset allergy [10][46]. The debate over whether epigenetic clocks are "useful" is recast as a partition[7][58]: clocks index a real, differently coupled dimension (pneumonia, mortality-adjacent biology[1][2][3]; and, for the asthma-nasal-polyp endpoint only, a weak allergic coupling); the allergy-relevant dimension is functional [4][5][50]. Any intervention cohort in which clock responses and haematological responses move in lock-step would falsify this partition.

**Second, the process model**: allergic propensity is a regulated set-point. Its level drifts 0.074 SD per decade from early adulthood in cross-sectional data (0.070 after birth-cohort adjustment; no period level shift; steeper, not shallower, in the later period, each decomposition direction arguing against cohort inflation). It mildly accelerates after midlife, keeps its damping through adult life (resilience loss confined to end-of-life in HRS)[49][28], and carries an age-invariant risk gain that the full index confirms against objective IgE, an eosinophil-carried objective signal, whereas the self-reported association is additionally eosinophil-independent. Later-life allergy is therefore the cumulative read-out of a lifelong, blood-count-visible drift, not a late-onset transition. The model separates two easily conflated specificities: the drift is haematologically general (myeloid skew[59]; eosinophil and neutrophil components drift alike), while the allergic-risk gain is type-2 specific. Three registered predictions distinguish the model in incident data: within-person drift rate predicts incident allergy; early-warning dispersion signals precede onset; the gain re-estimates unchanged.

**Third, a translational node with mapped boundaries**: IL18 is platform robust (meta OR 1.19) and barrier-resident[53][52][38]. It is framed mechanistically by a complete, compartmentalized ATP-inflammasome machinery, and it is absent from the curated Aging Atlas catalogue (KB01); that absence is the novelty the knowledge layer makes explicit. The honest negative, that the machinery's transcriptional regulation does not colocalize with allergy, redirects causal thinking from expression genetics to activation biology (ATP thresholds, inflammasome assembly) [52][53], precisely what organoid perturbation can test; the mirror-image nominal chains give those experiments a prior to falsify. IL18's safety map (directionally concordant off-targets) and druggability (tadekinig alfa) make it the lead node; TNFSF14 (dual allergy-ageing, but single-instrument-dependent[60], with infection and bone flags) is the cautious second.

**Fourth, a safety doctrine for the longevity era**: geroprotection and barrier-immune homeostasis trade off measurably. Only two screened compounds clear both curated filters, metoprolol, the prioritizable geroprotector-class candidate (LINCS single-resource, pending independent confirmation), and the tool compound LY-294002, while the internal-definition screen adds azithromycin as the prioritizable anti-alarmin-class candidate; the two define the combination logic rather than compete. Single-agent geroprotection will tend to carry a barrier-immune price; senolytic-plus-anti-alarmin combination design is the logical counter. This doctrine is currently hypothesis-grade: it rests on one perturbation resource and is signature-definition sensitive, as the quercetin case demonstrates.

### Limitations
Stated compactly, not minimized. (i) The frailty instrument is HLA dominated and carries direction only; magnitude claims attach to the outcome-informed composite, whose specificity is partly constructed. (ii) Effect magnitudes are heterogeneous across cohorts (I² = 90–99%); replication establishes direction, not point magnitude. (iii) The eosinophil decomposition is cohort dependent for asthma (unresolved); the atopic-dermatitis dimension contrast is convention dependent; cross-ancestry support is marginal and estimator-sensitive[61][47], and target nominations are European anchored. (iv) NHANES is cross sectional with self-reported main outcomes (objective IgE included); birth-cohort and age effects cannot be fully separated with two cycles (the two cycles also differ in drift slope, 0.056 vs 0.089, so a fraction of the gradient may be period/cohort composition), although direct decomposition found no period level shift, only ~5% attenuation after cohort adjustment, and no cohort-inflation direction; HRS measures no allergy; no incident-allergy cohort was available. (v) The single-cell layers rest on few donors, are nominal after family correction, and do not independently replicate the DICE IL4 colocalization; OneK1K implicated adjacent, not identical, genes. (vi) eQTLGen exposure effects were Z standardized; GTEx-lung tier covered two genes; machinery-gene pQTL awaits access-controlled platforms. (vii) Steiger audits showed only ~48% of frailty-instrument SNPs individually direction-correct for asthma (reversals concentrated at canonical immune loci already excluded), and the magnitude-carrying composite index was even less directionally resolvable (37% correct-direction for asthma; 19 Steiger-significant SNPs wrong versus 3 correct, binomial P = 0.017), so its odds ratios are reported as association magnitudes, not directional causal estimates; directionality rests on the filtered/non-HLA instrument and the MHC-free correlation, not per SNP. (viii) No wet-lab validation: every mechanistic-chain claim above nomination grade is a registered prediction, not a result.

### Conclusions
Allergic susceptibility tracks the functional dimension of ageing: a lifelong, measurably drifting haematological type-2 set-point (cross-sectional, cohort-checked) whose risk effect does not amplify with age, whose adult-onset preference is written in shared genetic architecture, and whose barrier interface resolves to a druggable inflammasome-IL-18 node absent from the curated Aging Atlas catalogue. The coupling map, the formal drift model and the IL18 mechanism chain convert "allergy and ageing" from correlation into a testable, druggable framework, and they hand the longevity field a safety constraint it has not yet priced in.
