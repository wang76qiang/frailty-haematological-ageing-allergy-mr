## Supplementary Note 2. Known limitations and sensitivity statement

Thirteen limitations are declared for completeness. First, cross-cohort effect-size heterogeneity is substantial (I2 = 90-99% in all three replication meta-analyses; for atopic dermatitis the smaller EAGLE cohort was discordant at OR 0.75, P = 0.27, while the pre-specified primary source (the Budu-Aggrey EUR meta-analysis, restored after an initial disk-related omission) replicated decisively at OR 2.66, 2.45-2.89), so replication establishes direction and robustness rather than a precise magnitude; LD score regression correlations requested in earlier rounds are now provided (ST59-ST60) and corroborate the frailty-versus-clock adjudication. Second, the OneK1K single-cell eQTL resource initially showed an unresolved instrument-harmonization discrepancy; the R7 remediation resolved this as a coverage/power property (DICE target genes only partially represented in OneK1K; exact lead SNP present in 7 of 33 pairs) and added independent-instrument analyses whose FDR-significant signals involve a different gene set (TNF, IL6, GATA3, RORC); cell-type MR claims rest on DICE, with OneK1K reported as evidence for an adjacent cell-gene architecture rather than corroboration of the DICE IL4-axis findings (Supplementary Note 5). Third, positive-control cis-pQTL MR used single-SNP instruments (F approximately 10-13) and is reported as underpowered and inconclusive rather than falsifying. Fourth, pharmacovigilance analyses are spontaneous-report based and confounded by indication and market presence; the omalizumab immunosenescence signal is explicitly flagged for confounder-adjusted interpretation and is not claimed as a class effect. Fifth, no incident-allergy cohort was available at analysis time: all allergic-outcome evidence is from lifelong case-control GWAS and prevalent survey data, so the type-2 set-point model is tested against prevalence rather than onset (a synthetic-validated incident-cohort pipeline awaits UK Biobank / All of Us extracts); within the three outcomes, the asthma and rhinitis arms now carry direction-level East-Asian support, whereas the atopic-dermatitis arm is bounded to European ancestry (Ninth, below). Sixth, the pQTL screen interrogates the circulating compartment, the positive-control type-2 cytokines were underpowered nulls, the on-target safety scan bounds lifelong genetic proxies rather than pharmacological modulation, and the mediation and gene-burden analyses were power-bounded nulls. Seventh, the target nominations are confined to atopic dermatitis and European ancestry, and the TNFSF14 replication is single-instrument-dependent and single-platform-type (rs413141 carries 71% of the inverse-variance weight; the cross-platform meta-analysis remains SomaScan-only, I2 = 68%; ST113, ST128); no target reached functional validation, so all target claims remain hypothesis-generating. Eighth, multi-signal colocalization at 5q31 used an approximation that can overstate PP.H4; the headline atopic-dermatitis signal survives conventional but not the strictest prior, and the rhinitis signal is single-SNP-driven (ST102); these are reported as suggestive. Ninth, cross-ancestry replication re-used European SNPs without East-Asian LD re-selection; its asthma/rhinitis support is marginal (fixed-effect FDR < 0.05), heterogeneous and estimator-sensitive (the random-effects and weighted-median estimators do not independently reproduce the asthma association; ST107), while the atopic-dermatitis association was direction-discordant and its cross-ancestry status is formally non-adjudicable because Biobank Japan atopic dermatitis fell below the heritability threshold for cross-population genetic-correlation estimation (Supplementary Note 8). Tenth, elevated cross-intercepts were observed among same-consortium trait pairs in the in-house LD score regression (clock-clock 0.34-0.47; allergy-allergy 0.16-0.34); although the headline frailty genetic correlations were reproduced by an independent implementation on the same summary statistics (ST126), these secondary-pair caveats remain. The reproduction is by a third-party Python-3 port rather than the canonical Bulik-Sullivan ldsc (Python-2-only and not runnable here), and the external benchmark (ST103) covers SNP heritability and clock-clock correlations but not the frailty-allergy correlations that carry the dimension contrast, so those correlations await independent canonical re-computation. Eleventh, pharmacovigilance signals derive from spontaneous reporting with channelling and indication biases, and the clinical-trial registry null (0 of 11,713 records) is a parsing/coverage property of the data dump rather than verified absence of reporting. Twelfth, many analyses (including the R7 and R8 evidence layers and the P2 cross-ancestry replication, the latter executed under a pre-written runbook but after the initial submission timeline) were added post hoc relative to the R6 analysis plan; no external pre-registration timestamp was obtained at analysis time, so the a priori property of the R5/R6 analyses rests on file provenance and Zenodo archival; and the genetic instruments were drawn predominantly from European-ancestry GWAS. Thirteenth, the genetic-level eosinophil decomposition (ST133) used a single outcome cohort (FinnGen); its independent replication in Pan-UK Biobank (ST136) reproduced the rhinitis result but did not reproduce the asthma result (the eosinophil-free asthma signal was positive and significant in UK Biobank, indicating that the FinnGen attenuation reflects limited power rather than eosinophil carriage), and the winner's-curse re-estimation re-uses panels that largely overlap the final index and is a stability check rather than an independent correction.

## Supplementary Note 3. Revision synthesis: reviewer critiques mapped to R5 analyses

This revision round responded to four survival-level critiques from the previous round. (i) Circularity of the "ageing" label (the composite index is blood-cell-based and ageing GWAS themselves depend on blood traits): addressed by five orthogonal ageing instruments and an analysis plan specified a priori, adjudicating path C, the frailty dimension, rather than the epigenetic clocks, is the dimension associated with allergic susceptibility. (ii) Winner's curse inflating the composite-index odds ratios: addressed by a three-sample design (Astle selection, Pan-UKBB estimation, FinnGen outcome); corrected and naive estimates were nearly identical (OR 1.678 vs 1.645), a stability check rather than an independent correction because the panels overlap (Limitations). (iii) Unweighted NHANES analyses overestimating effects: addressed by complex-survey-weighted estimation with cluster-robust standard errors; weighting shifted estimates by +8.5%, below the a priori 30% materiality threshold. (iv) The IL4 paradox (dupilumab efficacy versus apparent genetic IL4 protection): partially resolved by colocalization, showing whole-blood IL4 protection to be a linkage-disequilibrium artefact (PP.H3 approximately 0.97-1.00) while IL4 expression in naive regulatory T cells provides a candidate cell-type-specific signal (PP.H4 = 0.79-0.92 in the initial single-variant analysis; refined by R6 multi-signal colocalization and reported as hypothesis-generating; see Supplementary Note 4).

## Supplementary Note 4. R6 evidence-strengthening analyses

Six analyses specified a priori in an R6 analysis plan (since registered on the Open Science Framework, project osf.io/f4v79; registrations qm2hy (2026-09-20) and f9d3w (2026-09-25, post-hoc layers), both after the R6 analyses) closed the evidence gaps identified by external editorial feedback.

**(i) Independent replication (ST57-ST58).** The frailty effect replicated for asthma in all three independent sources (Han UK Biobank OR 2.25; TAGC OR 1.58; Neale OR 1.08; random-effects meta-analysis OR 2.39, 95% CI 1.63-3.50) and for rhinitis in UK Biobank Neale (OR 1.15); for atopic dermatitis, the pre-specified primary source (Budu-Aggrey EUR meta, 98 SNPs) replicated decisively (OR 2.66, 2.45-2.89; primary meta-analysis random-effects OR 1.97, 1.09-3.55, P = 0.024), while the smaller EAGLE cohort (a constituent of that meta-analysis) was discordant and imprecise (OR 0.75, P = 0.27, 17 SNPs) in the sensitivity scheme; the a priori decision rule is therefore satisfied for all three outcomes. The composite haematological type-2 index replicated across all six sources.

**(ii) LD score regression (ST59-ST60, ST76).** SNP heritabilities were well estimated for all eight traits; frailty showed strong positive genetic correlations with all three outcomes (rg = 0.458, 0.353 and 0.250; all q < 1e-13), whereas clock-outcome correlations were predominantly null, corroborating the MR adjudication with an instrument-independent method; cross-trait intercepts were approximately 0 (no sample overlap). The analysis set is MHC-free by construction (0 of 2,823 frailty HLA-window SNPs present; ST77), and the in-house implementation was validated by simulation calibration on the real LD-score scaffold (bias |0.005| or less, jackknife-SE/empirical-SD ratio 0.94-1.05, 95% coverage 0.93-0.96, type-I error 0.06-0.07 at the null; ST79); the official Python-2-era ldsc initially could not be executed on this platform; it has since been run under Python 3 with mechanical interpreter-compatibility patches only (no algorithmic change; Supplementary Note 10(viii); ST137-ST138). An external benchmark against published official-LDSR estimates (ST103; Supplementary Note 7) found near-exact agreement on identical input GWAS (frailty h2 0.111 vs 0.11 (SE 0.005), Atkins et al.; IEAA h2 0.164 vs 0.17 (SE 0.02), rg(GrimAge, PhenoAge) 0.672 vs 0.66, rg(GrimAge, IEAA) 0.243 vs 0.28, McCartney et al.) and directional/ordering concordance with UK Biobank allergic-disease rg estimates.

**(iii) Multivariable MR and instrument sensitivity (ST61-ST65).** The frailty effect survived adjustment for BMI, smoking initiation and education (asthma OR 2.68, P = 1.9e-9; atopic dermatitis OR 1.79, P = 8.9e-8; rhinitis borderline under the LD-GLS estimator, P = 0.060) and exclusion of all canonical allergy/immune loci (86/99 SNPs removed; asthma OR essentially unchanged at 3.43; formal full-versus-excluded consistency z-test P = 0.99 for asthma, 0.48 for atopic dermatitis and 0.013 for rhinitis with the excluded estimate larger; ST78); joint multivariable modelling showed mutually independent conditional effects of frailty and the composite index on all three outcomes.

**(iv) 5q31 multi-signal colocalization and SMR/HEIDI (ST66-ST69).** Stepwise conditional signal resolution with shadow masking refined the Treg-IL4 claim: the protective colocalization localizes to the rs6879672 LD block and survives for atopic dermatitis (matched-pair PP.H4 = 0.921, approximate and upward-biased by significance-only eQTL statistics) and suggestively for asthma (0.865, GWAS-subthreshold), while the rhinitis colocalization is single-SNP-driven (rs187681) and is reported as suggestive; the whole-blood IL4 null was strengthened (all 70 per-signal tests PP.H3-dominant); SMR was uniformly protective in direction but HEIDI failed in 5 of 6 tests, expected under the demonstrated multi-signal architecture, so adjudication rests on per-signal colocalization. A prior-sensitivity recomputation (ST102; see Supplementary Note 7) showed the atopic-dermatitis PP.H4 survives conventional priors (>= 0.8 for p12 >= 5e-6; range 0.85-0.99 across p12 = 5e-6 to 1e-4 and outcome prior variances 0.15^2-0.2^2) but attenuates to 0.54 at the strictest p12 = 1e-6, while the asthma signal (0.795 at baseline) falls below 0.8 at p12 <= 1e-5, confirming its suggestive status.

**(v) NHANES index validity (ST70-ST73).** The index rises with chronological age (cycle-specific weighted r = 0.20 and 0.24; pooled r = 0.22, P = 6.2e-83; monotone across age decades), tracks log-CRP beyond its mechanical CRP component, and carries incremental predictive value over age for allergic outcomes (Wald P down to 1.2e-11; delta-AIC > 60 for the composite in both cycles), supporting the "ageing" label while the age-invariant effect (no age-by-index interaction) refines the set-point interpretation.

**(vi) Targeted on-target safety MR scan (ST74-ST75).** Scanning the IL18 and TNFSF14 cis-pQTL instruments across ten non-allergic FinnGen R12 endpoints in five safety categories found IL18's off-target-endpoint associations directionally concordant with its atopic-dermatitis risk effect (higher IL18: psoriasis OR 1.24, q = 2.1e-4; femur fracture OR 1.19, q = 0.006, predicting no adverse signal from IL18 blockade at these endpoints), while higher TNFSF14 associated with psoriasis (OR 1.11, q = 0.034) but lower septicaemia (OR 0.92) and femur-fracture (OR 0.90) risk, flagging infection and bone health for surveillance of TNFSF14-directed suppression. These are lifelong genetic proxies that bound, not equate to, pharmacological on-target risk.

**Documented deviations from the R6 plan:** the Budu-Aggrey atopic-dermatitis source was omitted at the first R6-01 download pass owing to local disk constraints and was restored on 2026-09-12 for the editorial M1 revision, after which the atopic-dermatitis adjudication was updated (from downgraded to retained); the LDSC pipeline reports all 28 pairwise rg (the pre-specified 15-pair FDR family is additionally reported and no conclusion differs); the Neale 6152_9 endpoint combines hayfever/allergic rhinitis with eczema; and NHANES likelihood-based comparisons used weights normalized to mean 1 (cluster-robust Wald inference is scale-invariant).

## Supplementary Note 5. R7 evidence-elevation analyses

Seven R7 modules extended the evidence base; completed real-data analyses are reported here, and blocked modules are declared without fabrication. The R7 analyses were undertaken post hoc after the pre-specified plan and the R6 analysis plan, motivated by editorial feedback; they are hypothesis-refining and are reported as such (no R7 module alters the a priori adjudication).

**(i) Phenome-wide MR specificity scan (ST80-ST82; Supplementary Fig. 2).** Both ageing exposures were scanned against 17 FinnGen R12 endpoints (34 tests, 20 FDR-significant). The composite haematological type-2 index was specific to allergic endpoints (asthma OR 2.34, q = 7.7e-5; atopic dermatitis OR 1.88, q = 7.0e-7; rhinitis OR 1.63, q = 3.1e-3; asthma-nasal-polyp composite OR 2.00, q = 8.6e-8), with only a modest inverse myocardial-infarction association (OR 0.81, q = 0.024) outside the allergic category. The frailty instrument was broadly pleiotropic and HLA-driven (rheumatoid arthritis OR 477; type 1 diabetes OR 6.6e5; psoriasis OR 3.29; depression OR 1.91; myocardial infarction OR 2.36; femur fracture OR 2.34; pneumonia OR 1.22; ulcerative colitis OR 0.17; chronic nasopharyngeal disease OR 0.27), quantifying its pleiotropy risk and supporting the narrowing of magnitude claims to the index. Outlier-corrected IVW left the index allergy estimates unchanged (asthma 2.34, rhinitis 1.63, atopic dermatitis 1.89), whereas the extreme frailty autoimmune estimates were outlier-sensitive.

**(ii) HRS longitudinal validation (ST83-ST86; Supplementary Fig. 7).** In RAND HRS 1992-2022 (45,234 participants, 16 waves, 723,744 person-waves; the mortality analysis set comprised 36,528 participants with 18,276 deaths, ST83), the deficit-accumulation frailty index predicted all-cause mortality (HR 1.61 per SD of 0.131, 1.59-1.63) and incident lung disease (1.77), heart disease (1.59), stroke (1.57), diabetes (1.48) and cancer (1.16); a time-varying Cox specification gave HR 1.85 per SD (1.83-1.87, recomputed from ST84 at SD = 0.131). The mortality effect attenuated mildly with age (stratified HRs 1.72 / 1.55 / 1.43 per SD at 50-65 / 65-80 / > 80 years, recomputed from ST85 at SD = 0.131; FI x age HR 0.991 per SD-year, P = 3e-44). Early-warning-signal analyses found higher within-person FI variance (median 0.0065 vs 0.0025) and lag-1 autocorrelation (0.53 vs 0.38, both P < 1e-116) in participants who later died, a critical-slowing signature (values from the corrected death-flag mapping of the companion re-analysis, superseding the initial 0.0059/0.0026 and 0.55/0.45 estimates in ST86). These estimates survived the pre-specified mean-FI adjustment (variance adjusted P < 10⁻³⁰⁰; autocorrelation adjusted P = 3.1 × 10⁻116; ST105), and component-exclusion refits shifted incident-disease HRs by at most 4% (ST104), excluding mechanical self-prediction and mean-variance-coupling explanations (Supplementary Note 7). Hard limitation: RAND HRS contains no allergy outcomes, blood-cell traits, CRP or genotypes (0 hits across 19,880 variable labels), so incident-allergy and haematological-index analyses cannot be built from these files; a synthetic-validated generic pipeline awaits UK Biobank / All of Us extracts.

**(iii) OneK1K remediation and independent cell-type MR (ST87-ST88; Supplementary Fig. 8).** Streaming the six full OneK1K cis-eQTL tables showed the previously unresolved discrepancy is a coverage/power property, not a fixable harmonization bug: cis-eQTL records exist for GATA3 (5,514), IL1R1 (4,157), TSLP (3,415), IL4 (3,354) and STAT6 (2,244), but not for IL13, IL5 or COL1A1; the exact DICE lead SNP is present in only 7 of 33 significant pairs, with gene-level direction concordant in 3 of 7 (chance). An independent-instrument design (each gene's own OneK1K cis lead eSNP per cell type; 39 instruments, per-instrument first-stage F = 5.1-23.2 from rho at n = 982 donors; 111 Wald-ratio tests, 11 genes) yielded 14 FDR-significant associations independent of DICE: NK GATA3 protective for atopic dermatitis (OR 0.60, P = 1.2e-11) and asthma (OR 0.67); monocyte TNF risk-increasing for asthma (OR 1.69, P = 5.1e-10); B-cell IL6 risk-increasing for asthma (OR 1.50, P = 1.4e-5) and atopic dermatitis (OR 1.30); CD4-naive RORC protective. FDR-significant genes: TNF (6 tests), IL6 (4), GATA3 (2), RORC (2). The 14 FDR-significant tests span single-instrument F = 9.5-23.2; two sit marginally below the conventional F > 10 threshold (TNF rs2229094 in CD8 T cells, F = 9.5; IL6 rs61480128 in CD8 T cells, F = 10.0) and are flagged per-test in ST88. Single-lead Wald ratios are pleiotropy-sensitive; moreover, the FDR-significant OneK1K signals involve a different gene set (TNF, IL6, GATA3, RORC) than the DICE headline pairs (IL4, TSLP, IL5), so they are reported as independent instruments pointing to an adjacent but partly distinct cell-gene architecture, not as corroboration of the DICE IL4-axis findings.

**(iv) Blood-cell-trait decomposition MR (ST89; Supplementary Fig. 5).** Eosinophil-count instruments (167 SNPs, F = 85.5) drive the allergic signal (asthma OR 1.61, P = 5.4e-65; rhinitis OR 1.30, P = 6.5e-25; atopic dermatitis OR 1.27, P = 4.3e-37); total white-cell instruments (175 SNPs, F = 67.4) are weak for asthma (OR 1.10, P = 0.002) and null for rhinitis and atopic dermatitis. This is a component-attribution (falsification) analysis: because eosinophilia is itself a type-2 disease correlate, it rules out a nonspecific-leukocytosis explanation of the index but is not independent mechanistic evidence; reverse MR (allergy -> blood traits) bounds feedback at >= 8.9-fold below forward effects. Pan-UKBB chr:pos-keyed traits (CRP, lymphocyte percentage) could not be rsID-matched and were not analysed.

**(v) Single-cell ageing atlas (ST90-ST92; Supplementary Fig. 10).** Tabula Sapiens 2.0 blood/lung/large-intestine/small-intestine datasets (CZ CELLxGENE, streamed from CSR matrices, <= 40,000 cells per tissue, 43 signature genes) with donor-clustered robust inference gave 168 of 463 cell-type x module and 158 of 428 cell-type x gene age slopes at FDR < 0.05 (e.g., senescence-associated secretory phenotype module rising in plasma cells at 0.0042/year; alarmin module in CD8 T cells; IL18 in B cells and monocytes). Critical caveat: donor coverage is thin and age ranges narrow (blood 9 donors aged 33-69; intestine 5 donors aged 37-61; lung 4 donors aged 59-61 with essentially no age gradient), so results are descriptive/hypothesis-generating, not a validated ageing clock; erythrocyte signals likely reflect ambient-RNA contamination. Donor-level proportion anchoring of the haematological type-2 index was null and underpowered (all P > 0.37, n = 9 donors) and no anchoring claim is made.

**(vi) IL18/TNFSF14 cell-of-origin localization (ST93; Supplementary Fig. 13).** IL18 detection concentrated in myeloid compartments (lung macrophage 75%, colon macrophage 75%, alveolar type-1 cells 72%, blood intermediate monocytes 63%), consistent with an inflammageing origin; TNFSF14 concentrated in lymphoid/innate compartments (small-intestinal gamma-delta T cells 29%, natural killer cells 19%, blood neutrophils 17%), supporting the drug-target narrative with a cell-of-origin statement.

**(vii) Disease signature transfer (descriptive only; Supplementary Fig. 9).** In allergen-challenge airway single-cell data (GSE193816; Alladina et al., Sci. Immunol. 2023; 52,152 cells; all 43 signature genes present), cell-level ageing-module scores correlated with Th2 scores (r = 0.178) but not with alarmin scores (r = 0.007). Group labels (allergic asthma AA vs allergic control AC) and condition labels (baseline Bln, diluent Dil, allergen Ag) were confirmed against the publication's conventions on 2026-09-17; cluster-level group tests nonetheless treat cells as independent (pseudoreplication), so this module remains descriptive and no inferential claim is made. Cell-level outputs are retained in the code repository.

**Blocked modules (declared, not run):** the ageing-tool library upgrade required external summary statistics (DunedinPACE, healthspan, parental longevity, telomere) that are not locally present (0 of 4 files), so no new MR was run; longevity-instrument MR was blocked by a genome-build mismatch (chr:pos versus rsID) requiring liftover.

## Supplementary Note 6. R8 in silico substitution analyses

Four R8 modules substituted population-scale genetic evidence for the two blocked resources (UKB/All of Us incident cohorts; wet-lab experiments), each declared post hoc and hypothesis-refining.

**(i) Onset-stratified asthma MR and LDSC (ST94-ST98; Supplementary Fig. 3).** Ferreira 2019 onset-stratified asthma GWAS (adult-onset 26,582 cases; childhood-onset 13,962; 300,671 shared controls) were tested with the frailty instrument and the composite index: both exposures were directionally positive in both strata (frailty adult-onset OR 9.99, P = 1.3e-95; childhood-onset OR 24.6, P = 2.5e-63; index adult 2.23, childhood 4.38). Raw per-SD effects were larger for childhood-onset (contrast P = 4.3e-5 and 0.0059), mirroring its approximately threefold higher SNP heritability (observed-scale h2: adult 0.039, childhood 0.067); on the scale-free rg scale the frailty dimension shared more than twice the architecture with adult-onset disease (rg 0.487 vs 0.209; conservative contrast z = 5.83, P = 5.7e-9; clocks null/weak for both strata). Caveats: the strata share controls (contrast tests are conservative under independence), and the rg contrast treats the two correlations as independent (conservative because they share the frailty trait).

**(ii) Molecular mediation MR (ST99; Supplementary Fig. 15).** The IL18 and TNFSF14 cis-pQTL instruments were tested against 44 other inflammatory plasma proteins from the same Olink meta-analysis (Zhao et al. 2023) (90 tests): none passed FDR < 0.05 (the nominal IL18-to-IL33 trend, P = 0.017, did not). Interpretation: no detectable systemic circulating cascade downstream of the two targets, consistent with tissue-local action of myeloid IL18 and with the circulating-compartment limitation; this bounds rather than excludes tissue-level cascades.

**(iii) Tissue-context eQTL analysis (ST100; Supplementary Fig. 16).** In GTEx v8 EUR, none of IL4, IL13, IL5, TSLP or IL33 had a significant whole-blood cis-eQTL pair, whereas barrier/immune tissues showed strong regulation (IL4: 71 pairs in lung; TSLP: 385 esophagus, 350 skin, 182 spleen; IL33: 370 skin, 256 cultured fibroblasts; IL18: 229 skin-sun-exposed, 134 lung). This tissue-context pattern reinforces that whole-blood readouts of type-2 genes mis-specify the disease-relevant context (the IL4 paradox resolution); GTEx whole blood is far smaller than eQTLGen, so absence partly reflects power.

## Supplementary Note 10. Post-hoc strengthening analyses (Plan A/B)

Post-hoc analyses strengthen the drug-target, disease-spectrum and construct-validity layers; none alters the a priori adjudication.

**(i) Cross-platform pQTL replication of IL18 and TNFSF14 (ST127-ST128).** The HELIC Olink cohort (Gilly et al. 2020; GCST90010141), previously not estimable because the frozen instrument coordinates are GRCh37 and HELIC is GRCh38, was resolved by mapping the three IL18 instrument SNPs to GRCh38 coordinates: all three were direction-concordant and the fixed-effect IVW replicated the IL18 atopic-dermatitis effect (OR 1.22, P = 1.1e-8). A cross-platform inverse-variance meta-analysis now combines three IL18 platforms (SCALLOP Olink, Ahola-Olli Luminex, HELIC Olink; pooled OR 1.19, 95% CI 1.15-1.23, P = 3.6e-23, I2 = 0%) and two TNFSF14 platforms (AGES and INTERVAL SomaScan; pooled OR 1.13, 1.05-1.22, P = 8.8e-4, I2 = 68%). IL18 therefore rests on three cross-platform replications using the same three cis instruments, of which only the HELIC (Olink) and Ahola-Olli (Luminex) layers are independent of the discovery meta-analysis (SCALLOP Consortium); TNFSF14 remains single-platform-type and rests on a single independent layer (AGES; INTERVAL is a discovery constituent). The deCODE SomaScan and UK Biobank-PPP layers remain access-blocked (captcha form and Synapse token respectively) and the per-mask UK Biobank-exome gene-burden odds ratios sit behind the Open Targets Genetics portal (unreachable in the analysis environment); as a proxy, gnomAD loss-of-function constraint is reported in ST129 (IL18 LoF score 0.32, i.e. not strongly LoF-intolerant; IL33 approximately 0).

**(ii) Type-2 disease-spectrum MR (ST130).** The 101-SNP composite index instrument was tested against independent UK Biobank endpoints (Pan-UK Biobank, EUR): asthma (icd10-J45) OR 2.78 (P = 5.4e-100), allergic rhinitis (phecode 476) OR 1.58 (P = 0.023) and urticaria (icd10-L50) OR 1.04 (P = 0.92), with null estimates at the non-type-2 negative controls type 1 diabetes (OR 0.85, P = 0.24) and rheumatoid arthritis (OR 1.10, P = 0.39). The index therefore replicates positively for asthma and rhinitis in UK Biobank, is null for urticaria, and retains specificity against non-type-2 autoimmune endpoints. Additional externally downloaded type-2 endpoints (eosinophilic esophagitis, chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps) could not be retrieved within the analysis environment (throughput-limited GWAS Catalog transfer) and remain to be added.

**(iii) Non-HLA frailty re-adjudication (ST131).** To test whether the frailty-allergy signal is carried by HLA content, the frailty instrument was rebuilt from the 13 of 99 instrument SNPs that lie outside every canonical immune locus (ST02 minus ST63) and tested as a non-HLA instrument against FinnGen R12 and Pan-UK Biobank outcomes: asthma OR 3.85 (P = 2.0e-13, FinnGen) and OR 2.38 (P = 4.6e-12, Pan-UKB), allergic rhinitis OR 2.67 (FinnGen), atopic dermatitis OR 1.60 (FinnGen), with the UK Biobank rhinitis and urticaria estimates null. The association therefore survives complete removal of immune-locus content, so the frailty dimension's link to allergy is not an HLA artefact. **(iv) External healthspan dimension re-adjudication (ST132).** To provide an independent, non-HLA, powered ageing exposure, the Timmers 2019 healthspan GWAS (GCST007406; n = 300,447 British-ancestry individuals) was retrieved; after excluding the MHC window (chr6:25-34 Mb), 13 genome-wide-significant SNPs were available as an instrument. Against the FinnGen outcomes this external instrument showed a nominal, direction-consistent protective association with asthma (OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.53-0.86, P = 1.2e-3; I2 = 0%) and null estimates for allergic rhinitis (OR 0.86, P = 0.17) and atopic dermatitis (OR 0.93, P = 0.37). Because healthspan is a positive-valence measure, a protective OR is the direction that mirrors the frailty-risk axis (worse ageing, higher allergy), so this provides an external non-HLA anchor for the ageing direction in asthma, albeit with two caveats: the association is nominal (not FDR-corrected across outcomes) and the healthspan signal is cardiometabolically weighted (for example TCF7L2, CDKN2B, SH2B3), so it may partly reflect metabolic rather than intrinsic ageing biology. A DunedinPACE GWAS remained unavailable.

**(v) Genetic-level eosinophil decomposition of the composite index (ST133, ST136).** To test whether the composite index's allergic associations are carried by its dominant eosinophil component (weight 0.50) at the genetic level, the forward MR against the three FinnGen R12 outcomes was re-run with the 17 eosinophil-percentage SNPs removed (73 harmonized SNPs), alongside the full (88 SNP) and eosinophil-only (15 SNP) instruments under identical allele-aware harmonization (palindromic SNPs with outcome allele frequency 0.42-0.58 dropped). The reported mean first-stage F of approximately 1,229 is the mean per-SNP F = (beta/SE)^2 computed in the large Pan-UKBB component scans in which these SNPs were selected at genome-wide significance; it therefore certifies selection strength in the selection GWAS (and is winner's-curse inflated as a selection statistic) and is not comparable to the frailty/clock instrument F values computed in their smaller source GWAS. A first-stage F comparable to the frailty/clock instruments cannot be defined for the index because its per-SNP effects are estimated in the selection scans rather than in the outcome GWAS; the index MR estimates are therefore presented with explicit weak-instrument caution and are accompanied by MR-Egger and weighted-median sensitivity (ST133). The full instrument reproduced the archived estimates almost exactly (asthma OR 2.34 versus 2.32 archived), validating the re-harmonization. The eosinophil-free instrument retained the atopic-dermatitis association (OR 2.02, P = 2.0e-9; random-effects P = 5.9e-3; weighted-median OR 3.37) but was attenuated to nominal significance for asthma (OR 1.42, P = 0.046; random-effects P = 0.45; I2 = 86%) and null for allergic rhinitis (OR 0.80, P = 0.17), whereas the eosinophil-only instrument was strongly associated with all three outcomes (OR 1.85-2.59; random-effects P <= 9.7e-4). All three harmonization rules examined (dropping ambiguous palindromes, frequency-oriented retention, as-is retention) gave eosinophil-free asthma estimates of OR 1.37-1.42, so the attenuation is not an artefact of palindromic handling; an earlier internal r3 sensitivity estimate (asthma OR 2.81, n = 84) was not allele-aware (it included an allele-mismatched SNP and SNPs unmatched in the outcome) and is superseded by this analysis; the superseded estimate was stronger than the full-index estimate across all three outcomes (asthma 2.81 versus 2.32; rhinitis 1.86 versus 1.62; atopic dermatitis 2.47 versus 1.90), a divergence consistent with allele-mismatch contamination rather than with genuine eosinophil-free carriage. Replication in an independent cohort (Pan-UK Biobank; ST136) applied the identical three instruments under identical harmonisation to UK Biobank asthma (icd10-J45), allergic rhinitis (phecode 476) and urticaria (icd10-L50): the full index associated with asthma (OR 2.79) and rhinitis (OR 1.57); the eosinophil-free instrument lost the rhinitis association (OR 1.53, P = 0.39) but was positive and significant for asthma (OR 1.86, P = 1.7e-7; random-effects P = 0.034; weighted median 2.74), so the FinnGen eosinophil-free asthma attenuation (OR 1.42, random-effects P = 0.45) reflects limited power rather than eosinophil carriage and the asthma result did not replicate; the eosinophil-only instrument was strongly associated with both (OR 3.03 and 1.58) and the urticaria control was null. Interpretation: the rhinitis association is predominantly eosinophil-carried and the atopic-dermatitis association includes a substantial non-eosinophil genetic signal (FinnGen; atopic dermatitis is not available in Pan-UK Biobank), whereas the asthma decomposition did not replicate (the eosinophil-free instrument was positive in both cohorts and significant only in the larger), so no eosinophil-carrying claim is made for asthma; this contrasts with the phenotype level (ST124), where an eosinophil-free index remained associated with self-reported allergy (OR 1.63) but not with total IgE.

**(vi) Formal frailty-versus-clock genetic-correlation contrasts (ST134).** The dimension adjudication was formalized by testing rg(Frailty, outcome) minus rg(clock, outcome) for each of the four clocks and three outcomes (12 contrasts, BH-FDR; z on the difference with SE under a conservative independence assumption on the shared-outcome sampling covariance; a rho = 0.5 correlated sensitivity gives stronger contrasts). For asthma, the frailty correlation (0.458) significantly exceeded every clock (GrimAge 0.165, Hannum 0.113, IEAA 0.083, PhenoAge 0.082; differences 0.29-0.38, all q <= 4.3e-4, 95% CIs non-overlapping); for allergic rhinitis likewise (frailty 0.353 versus clocks -0.05 to 0.04; all q <= 1.4e-3). For atopic dermatitis the frailty correlation (0.250) did not significantly exceed any clock, and the PhenoAge correlation (0.274) was nominally larger (difference -0.02, P = 0.83). The independent Python-3 LDSC implementation (ST126) reproduced the same pattern (asthma q <= 5.8e-4; rhinitis q <= 1.8e-3; atopic dermatitis all null). The dimension-level advantage of the functional dimension is therefore supported under the two non-canonical implementations used here for asthma and rhinitis but not for atopic dermatitis, whose dimension attribution remains unresolved; this outcome-specificity is now stated in the abstract, Results and Conclusions.

**(vii) Formal Steiger directionality audit (ST135, per-SNP detail ST135b).** Editorial review noted that the reverse-MR bound (reverse effects at least 8.9-fold below forward effects on the log-OR scale) is a cross-scale magnitude comparison, not a formal directionality test, and that the reverse chain tests allergic outcomes against blood-cell traits rather than against ageing itself. A formal SNP-level Steiger variance-explained audit of the forward analysis (5 ageing instruments + composite index x 3 FinnGen outcomes; R2 = 2 x EAF x (1-EAF) x beta^2 per side, orientation-free; Fisher-z contrast with per-SNP effective N) shows: (a) every clock-instrument SNP explains more variance in its exposure than in the outcome across all three outcomes (100% correct direction, all Steiger-significant; sum-R2 ratios 14-3953); (b) the frailty instrument is only 48% correct-direction for asthma, with the median SNP explaining more variance in the outcome than in the frailty exposure and, among Steiger-significant SNPs, ten wrong-direction versus two correct (all at canonical allergy/immune loci), so single-SNP direction for frailty-asthma is largely undeterminable; the frailty-asthma direction rests instead on the MHC-free genome-wide genetic correlation (ST59-ST60, ST126) and the non-HLA instrument re-adjudication (ST131), not on individual SNPs; for rhinitis and atopic dermatitis the frailty instrument was predominantly correct-direction (80% and 72%); (c) for the composite index, 29-37% of SNPs explain more variance in the outcome than in the index, the expected signature of blood-cell SNPs residing in allergy loci. Interpretation: the forward direction is formally supported for the clock instruments and, for the frailty instrument, only at the genome-wide (MHC-free) level, not at the single-SNP level for asthma; allergy-to-ageing feedback remains untested directly because the reverse outcomes are blood-cell traits, not ageing. The main text and limitation Fourth now state this explicitly, and the 8.9-fold comparison is labelled as descriptive.

**(viii) Canonical LDSC re-computation and MHC sensitivity (ST137-ST138).** The frailty-versus-clock dimension contrast was re-computed with the canonical Bulik-Sullivan implementation (ldsc v1.0.1) under the standard protocol: munge_sumstats.py on the published GWAS (McCartney clock GWAS GCST90014288-92; Atkins frailty GCST90020053; FinnGen R12 outcomes; w_hm3.snplist allele merge), then ldsc.py --rg on the published 1000 Genomes Phase 3 HapMap3 weights with the MHC excluded (weights.hm3_noMHC;.l2.M derived as per-chromosome reference SNP counts; --not-M-5-50, which scales h2 but leaves rg unchanged because the SNP-count factor cancels in the ratio). The original Python-2-era code required only mechanical interpreter-compatibility patches (xrange,.ix, print, map, tab alignment; no algorithmic change; patched tree and run logs archived under data/r6/canonical_ldsc). All fifteen correlations were recovered with absolute rg differences from the in-house engine of 0.001-0.086 (median 0.025); the frailty correlations were nearly identical (asthma 0.467 vs 0.458; rhinitis 0.361 vs 0.353; atopic dermatitis 0.246 vs 0.246; Supplementary Table 137). Formal contrasts under the canonical engine (same z-on-difference procedure as ST134, both SE conventions) reproduce the asthma (all q <= 2.6e-3, rho=0) and rhinitis (all q <= 2.1e-3) dimension advantages; for atopic dermatitis the contrast is convention-dependent (not significant under the conservative rho = 0 SE; significant for three of four clocks under the rho = 0.5 convention; Supplementary Table 138), refining rather than overturning the in-house boundary. An MHC-included sensitivity reference (LDscore.1kgPhase3.hm3) raised the frailty correlations by only 0.019-0.033 (asthma 0.492, rhinitis 0.380, atopic dermatitis 0.279; Supplementary Table 137), confirming at the genome-wide level that the frailty-allergy correlation is not carried by the HLA region.

**(ix) Donor-level single-cell expression-age analysis (ST139-ST145; Supplementary Figs. 11, 12, 14).** The full Tabula Sapiens 2.0 Immune atlas (592,317 cells; 24 donors; 22-74 years; 74 tissues; decontXcounts decontaminated counts) and the Lung atlas (65,847 cells; 3 donors aged 59-61) were analysed with the donor as the inferential unit. Per donor and cell type, gene counts were summed and divided by total counts (expression rate). **(xii) Formal set-point drift model (ST153-ST155, ST154b; Supplementary Fig. 4).** The composite-index dynamics were formalized as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck homeostat, X = S(age) + e, with S(age) = S0 + beta x (age - 50)/10 and stationary variance sigma2/(2 x lambda(age)); a declining-damping model lambda(age) = lambda0 x exp(-delta x (age - 50)/10) makes log residual variance linear in age with slope delta, so delta > 0 would be the cross-sectional signature of declining homeostatic resilience. The index was rebuilt from components (correlation 0.991 with the archived extract) in NHANES cycles D (2005-2006) and J (2017-2018); all models are survey-weighted (WTMEC2YR; pooled weights halved) with strata/PSU-clustered standard errors. Drift: the index rose at 0.074 SD per decade pooled (95% CI 0.060-0.088, P = 1.2e-25, n = 9,360), independently in cycle J (0.089, P = 5.1e-20) and cycle D (0.056, P = 1.3e-9), and at 0.066 after BMI adjustment (ST153); the cycle-specific rates differ descriptively (0.089 vs 0.056; overlapping CIs), plausibly reflecting period/cohort composition, and the pooled estimate is reported as primary; these adult (20+) estimates are shallower than the per-decade slope implied by the archived full-age-range correlation (r = 0.22), which additionally spans the steeper adolescent-to-adult maturation rise. Drift shape: a hinge model outperformed linear drift (best knot 55-60 years; pooled delta-AIC 18.3; cluster-bootstrap knot median 55, IQR 55-60, boundary fraction 0.04): the drift is stable but accelerates approximately threefold after age 55-60 (pooled pre-knot 0.039 versus post-knot 0.137 SD per decade; increment 0.098, P = 7.2e-4; per-cycle: J 0.057 to 0.188, D 0.024 to 0.111), with no plateau (ST155, ST156). Drift specificity: the drift is NOT type-2-specific -- eosinophil and neutrophil components drift comparably (pooled 0.039, P = 1.8e-8 versus 0.044, P = 8.8e-10 SD per decade; per-cycle concordant), and the equal-weight index replicates the drift (0.068, P = 6.1e-23) -- so the type-2 specificity of the index resides in its allergic-risk gain rather than in the drift itself (ST156). The full component panel recapitulates the canonical age-related myeloid-skew signature: lymphocytes drift down (-0.077 SD per decade, P = 7.0e-27), monocytes drift up (+0.081, P = 4.7e-24) and total white-cell count is essentially flat (-0.013, P = 0.013), so the index drift is a weighted sum of compositional myeloid skew rather than leukocytosis, converging with the genetic-layer decomposition (ST89) (ST156). Resilience: residual dispersion did not increase monotonically with age (delta = 0.000 per decade pooled, P = 0.99; delta = 0.022 in J, P = 0.29; delta = -0.028 in D, P = 0.19; sex-stratified and equal-weight-index sensitivities concordant), so the cross-sectional critical-slowing signature is absent through mid-adulthood; decade-bin dispersion is U-shaped (weighted Brown-Forsythe P = 0.007), elevated below 30 and above 70, which we report as a compositional observation without a resilience claim (ST154, ST154b). Re-estimating delta from the hinge-model residuals gave the same null (J 0.030, P = 0.13; D -0.032, P = 0.10; pooled 0.010, P = 0.49; ST154), so the absent dispersion trend is not an artefact of the linear mean specification. Gain invariance: the risk effect showed no age amplification for the J-cycle allergic composite (interaction P = 0.94) or for the objective D-cycle IgE outcomes (log-IgE interaction P = 0.14; high-IgE interaction nominally attenuating, beta = -0.062, P = 0.021, opposite in direction to amplification) (ST155). These cycle-specific, BMI-adjusted adult estimates complement the pooled full-age-range interaction of Supplementary Table 16 (weighted P = 0.394); the two families differ in outcome definition, age range and covariates, and concur in direction (no age amplification anywhere). Interpretation: the adult phase reads as set-point drift with intact damping and an age-invariant gain, with the critical-slowing signature documented in HRS decedents not visible in cross-sectional adult dispersion; cross-sectional age trends confound birth-cohort effects, so the drift parameters are population-level descriptions, and the model's incident-cohort predictions (drift rate predicts onset; early-warning signals precede onset; age-invariant gain re-estimates unchanged) remain to be tested.

## Supplementary Note 11. Fourth-round editorial revision (R35, 2026-10-09)

This note records six additions made in response to independent editorial review. No new empirical data were generated; every number derives from the already-deposited result tables.

**(i) Objective-IgE layer vs eosinophil decomposition (ST124; reconciles the main-text eosinophil-independence claim).** The main text reports that the full composite index predicts objective IgE (ST19: log-IgE beta 0.310; high-IgE OR 1.53) and, separately, that an eosinophil-free index retains the *self-reported* allergy association (ST124, J cycle: OR 1.63 composite vs 1.07 eosinophils-alone). ST124 shows these two layers do not coincide for IgE: in cycle D the eosinophil-free index is null for log-IgE (beta 0.005, P = 0.88) and high-IgE (OR 1.04, P = 0.45), whereas the eosinophil-only index carries the signal (log-IgE beta 0.351, P = 6.4e-25; high-IgE OR 1.53, P = 2.0e-20). The objective-IgE validation therefore certifies construct validity of the full type-2 index and is eosinophil-carried; eosinophil-independence is demonstrated for the self-reported association only. The main text (Results 3.4, 3.5; Discussion) now states this explicitly. The value discrepancy between ST19 (idx_pub log-IgE beta 0.310) and ST124 (idx_pub log-IgE beta 0.210) reflects differing covariate/weighting specifications, declared here.

**(ii) Composite-index Steiger audit (ST135).** The per-SNP Steiger audit was previously reported for the frailty instrument only. The magnitude-carrying composite index is the least directionally resolvable: for asthma, 37% of SNPs explain more variance in the index than in the outcome, and among Steiger-significant SNPs 19 point in the wrong direction versus 3 correct (binomial P = 0.017); frailty-instrument values were 48% (asthma), 80% (rhinitis), 72% (atopic dermatitis). Composite odds ratios are therefore reported as association magnitudes; direction rests on the MHC-free genetic correlation and the non-HLA instrument (ST131). Now stated in main-text limitation (vii).

**(iii) Knowledge-layer enrichment is deposited (KB01).** The 17-gene type-2/alarmin panel (IL4, IL5, IL13, TSLP, IL33, IL18, TNFSF14, RORC, IL6, TNF, GATA3, STAT6, IL1R1, IL1RL1, IL5RA, IL25, IL17RB; n = 17) overlaps the 500 curated Homo sapiens Aging Atlas genes (`data/real/aging_atlas/gene_sets_all.csv`) at 4 genes (IL1R1, IL6, TNF, TNFSF14; modules NF-kB-related, deregulated nutrient sensing, altered intercellular communication). Fisher enrichment against a ~20,018-gene protein-coding universe gives OR 12.1 (P = 7.1e-4); the NF-kB-related module (human n = 47) carries 2 panel genes (TNFSF14, IL1R1; OR 59.1, P = 7.1e-4). IL18 is absent from all 12 Aging Atlas modules. Membership, module sizes and universe conventions are deposited as KB01 (`KB01_aging_atlas_panel_enrichment.csv`), making the enrichment independently reproducible.

**(iv) Cross-sectional drift terminology (ST153).** The two NHANES cycles are samples of the same survey and are described as "reproduced across the two survey cycles" rather than "independently replicated"; cycle-specific rates (D 0.056; J 0.089) and the cycle x age interaction (0.033, P = 0.012) are reported as possible period/cohort composition, with the decomposition direction arguing against cohort inflation.

**(v) Clock directionality (ST01, ST58).** IEAA is significantly *protective* for asthma in both discovery and the replication meta-analysis (discovery OR 0.986, P = 3.2e-5; meta OR 0.990, P = 5.9e-5); PhenoAge is protective in discovery (OR 0.945) but attenuates in the meta (P = 0.081). This strengthens the "differently coupled dimension" reading (not "no clock effect"); now stated in the Results.

**(vi) Incident-allergy engine is synthetic (ST112).** ST112 is a *simulated* validation of the pre-registered incident-allergy Cox/competing-risk engine on data with a known per-SD hazard ratio of 1.30 and no age interaction (fitted HR 1.318); it contains no empirical cohort and is labelled SYNTHETIC in the table legend.
